# Cheap models win until tasks turn hard

For most bounded work in Claude Code, **Haiku 5.5 at low or medium effort matches the flawless output of the bigger models at about 1/20 to 1/45 of the cost**. That covers search and explore subagents, classification, extraction, SQL, test writing, short math and logic, strict-format output and small well-specified code changes. The picture flips on long, multi-step agentic work. There Haiku falls clearly short (Terminal-Bench 4.0: **36% against 64-65%**), and **Sonnet 5.5 is the cheapest model that ties Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2** on independent terminal-coding and aggregate tests. Opus 5.5 earns its price on hard code review, building whole programs and human-preferred UI work. Fable 5.2 is the only clear leader on business automation, science, long-horizon research and factual recall. Effort matters far more than the "use a bigger model" instinct suggests. On easy tasks it added cost and no score in the user's own 131-run sweep. On hard tasks it moved scores more than model choice did: Sonnet climbs from 26 to 55 on the Artificial Analysis index between low and max. Several settings are strictly dominated and should never be routed to: Sonnet xhigh and max, Opus low, high and max, and Fable xhigh. The evidence is uneven. Independent, primary-verified numbers exist for aggregate and agentic coding. Per-task-type data comes mostly from vendor tables, one-author tests and the user's own small sweep. Anthropic publishes no per-model weights for subscription limits, so API price ratios (**Haiku : Sonnet : Opus : Fable output = 1 : 20 : 40 : 80**) are the best stand-in for how fast each model burns a usage window ([pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)).

## Effort buys points only when the task is hard

On hard tasks, effort is the strongest lever. Artificial Analysis (AA) runs its Intelligence Index v5.0.1 at all five effort levels, and every score below was confirmed on AA's own model pages. The per-task cost is primary-verified only at max. The lower-effort costs come from a secondary write-up whose max values and scores match AA exactly ([AA Opus](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-opus-5-5), [AA Sonnet](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-sonnet-5-5), [AA Haiku](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-haiku-5-5), [AA Fable](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-fable-5-2), [Axentix](https://useaxentix.com/blog/llm/fable-5-2-vs-opus-5-5/), [laozhang.ai](https://blog.laozhang.ai/en/posts/claude-sonnet-5-5-vs-opus-5-5.md)).

| Effort | Haiku 5.5 | Sonnet 5.5 | Opus 5.5 | Fable 5.2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 19 (2.2 min) | 26 / $0.51 | 35 / $0.98 | **54 / $3.10** |
| medium | 25 (4.0 min) | 31 / $0.78 | **48 / $2.45** | **56 / $3.80** |
| high | 30 (6.5 min) | **42 / $1.64** | 52 / $3.46 | **62 / $9.08** |
| xhigh | 35 (9.9 min) | 50 / $4.09 | **57 / $6.05** | 62 / $14.83 |
| max | **36 / $0.41** | 55 / $10.02 | 59 / $10.04 | **65 / $26.54** |

*Index score / cost per index task. Bold = on the cost-quality frontier (no other setting scores higher for less money). Haiku costs below max are not published; $0.41 is an upper bound for its lower levels.*

The frontier explains the router's backbone. **Haiku max (36, $0.41) beats Opus low (35, $0.98) and every Sonnet setting below high.** Fable low (54, $3.10) beats Opus high (52, $3.46). Fable high (62, $9.08) beats Opus max (59, $10.04). Opus xhigh (57, $6.05) is cheaper than Sonnet max (55, $10.02) and scores higher. Sonnet max produced the most output tokens AA has recorded (**335M across the index against Opus max's 218M**), which erases its per-token price advantage. Haiku max also beats Sonnet low and medium on this index (36 against 26 and 31, at lower cost). That dominance holds on the index's hard mixed tasks, not on easy bounded work, where Sonnet low was flawless at summaries that Haiku got wrong (see the own-sweep slips below). Three plateaus recur across sources. Fable high and xhigh tie at 62, though xhigh uses 69% more output tokens. Haiku gains one point from xhigh to max. Sonnet can score *lower* at max than at xhigh. On FrontierCode it fell from 52.1% to 46.2% because it more often launched review subagents, hit timeouts or made out-of-scope edits ([Sonnet launch](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5)). Simon Willison watched Sonnet at max burn all 128,000 tokens and $1.28 without producing an answer, where xhigh finished in 41 seconds for under 6 cents ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/28/claude-sonnet-5-5/)).

The user's own sweep shows the other side. It ran 18 auto-graded text task types plus agent-tool tasks, 137 runs at $4.73 list price, one run per cell. **All four models scored at or near 100% at low effort, and raising effort added time and tokens, not points.** Mean cost per text run at low effort was **Haiku $0.003, Sonnet $0.033, Opus $0.061, Fable $0.130**. An 83 KB long-context retrieval cost $0.018 on Haiku and $0.989 on Fable, both correct. Haiku at max on hard math used four times the thinking and 3.5 times the time of low effort for the same 6/6 ([own sweep matrix](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-antigravity-setup/5b430f7e-0f7b-55c5-8d67-00dd071d0571/scratchpad/bench/sweep/summary.txt)). A separate one-author test agrees. Haiku 5.5 as a read-only Explore subagent matched ground truth in all 36 sessions at medium, for $0.016 against $0.621 for Opus medium. High and xhigh made it 1.8 to 2.8 times slower for no gain ([AIVio Labs](https://www.aiviolabs.com/en/blog/haiku-5-5-as-a-subagent)). The two pictures fit together: effort is a hard-task tool. The own tests are too easy to rank the top models. They set a floor and give cost ratios, nothing more.

Anthropic's own defaults agree with this reading. All four models default to medium in Claude Code ([model config](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config)). The effort guide says Fable "most of the gain is already present at high" and max often buys "only a small gain". It tells Haiku users to start at medium, use low for short high-volume work, and warns that at low in long agent prompts Haiku "is more likely to skip a search, stop early, or skip a check". For Sonnet it recommends medium for well-specified agentic coding and high for harder work ([effort docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort)).

## Independent agentic tests put Sonnet level with Opus and Fable

On the strongest independent coding evidence, the three larger models are tied and Sonnet is cheapest. Vals.ai's Terminal-Bench 4.0 run gives **Fable 65.15% ($14.56 per test), Opus 65.15% ($12.04), Sonnet 64.14% ($10.75) and Haiku 36.36% ±2.31 ($3.12)** ([Vals TB4](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/terminal-bench-4)). The effort settings are not listed. The top three sit within about one standard error, while Haiku trails by 28 points. Haiku's lower price ($8.58 per solved task against Sonnet's $16.76) does not matter under a quality-first rule, because the tasks it fails are exactly the ones the router most needs done. Snorkel's Terminal-Bench 4.1 card repeats the tie (65.8 / 65.8 / 63.9) ([Snorkel](https://snorkel.ai/leaderboard/os-world-2-0/)). The Vals Index, a broad agentic suite, gives **Fable 71.31% ±0.96 at $17.94, Sonnet 70.85% ±0.93 at $16.18, Opus 70.14% ±1.00 at $24.77, and Haiku 58.73% at $3.18** ([Vals Fable](https://www.vals.ai/models/anthropic_claude-fable-5-2), [Vals Sonnet](https://www.vals.ai/models/anthropic_claude-sonnet-5-5), [Vals Opus](https://www.vals.ai/models/anthropic_claude-opus-5-5), [Vals Haiku](https://www.vals.ai/models/anthropic_claude-haiku-5-5)). Again Sonnet ties the top for the least money, and Opus is the priciest of the three.

The ties break on specific task types. On **ProgramBench**, which builds whole programs, Fable scores 19.0% and Opus 18.5%, against Sonnet's 6.5% and Haiku's 2.5%. On **SRE Bench** Opus scores 33.6%, Sonnet 30.2% and Haiku 5.7%. On **Terminal-Bench-Science** Fable leads at 55.7%, with Opus and Sonnet both listed at 47.1% (a duplicate value that may be an extraction error) and Haiku at 10%. On **Vibe Code Bench**, small app builds, all four cluster at **90-94%, with Haiku (90.44%) level with Opus (90.29%)** (same Vals pages). This matches the user's own tests. Haiku at low completed a multi-file feature (20/20), data analysis, vision, mutation-tested test writing and spec-conflict judgment perfectly, and missed only one of two CLI tool-use checks, a miss not yet diagnosed ([own sweep handoff](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-antigravity-setup/5b430f7e-0f7b-55c5-8d67-00dd071d0571/scratchpad/model-analysis-handoff.md)). Haiku handles short, bounded, checkable coding and collapses on long-horizon operations work.

Zapier's AutomationBench, 657 multi-app business tasks graded strictly on end state, is the only independent full effort curve. In its Fable 5.2 report, **Fable scored 39.7% / 40.2% / 44.3% / 47.9% / 51.4% from low to max, at $1.30 to $4.64 per task**. Opus scored 23.3% to 40.0% ($0.46 to $1.28), and **Sonnet max scored 44.6% at $1.00** ([Zapier Fable report](https://zapier.com/benchmarks/claude-fable5_2-report)). Sonnet max beats Opus at every effort and ties Fable high for 40% less. Only Fable xhigh and max climb clearly higher. With 657 tasks the standard error is about ±2 points, so Fable max's 6.8-point lead over Sonnet is real. Its 3.5-point lead over Fable xhigh, at twice the cost, is borderline. Zapier's separate Opus report gives higher Opus costs ($1.62 to $4.22 per task, no-cache estimates) and **$6.95 to $10.54 per passed task**, so cost comparisons should stay within one report ([Zapier Opus report](https://zapier.com/benchmarks/claude-opus5_5-report)). Haiku was not tested.

Vendor tables fill the gaps but need discounting. Anthropic reports Sonnet 70.6% against Opus 66.4% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, while AA's independent run put Opus at 59.6% ([OrcaRouter on AA](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/claude-opus-5-5-coding-agent-index)). Anthropic's Fable launch table shows Fable ahead of Opus on most rows. The gaps are small on coding (FrontierCode 2.0: 49.1% vs 45.5%; OSWorld 2.1: 89.3 vs 87.2) and large on long-horizon and science work (Remote Labor Index 41.3% vs 21.3%; Terminal-Bench-Science 74.6 vs 58.7; HLE with tools 74.2 vs 67.7). Opus has the higher AA-Briefcase Elo (1800 vs 1785) ([Fable launch](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2)). On knowledge-work Elo, Sonnet and Opus tie (GDPval-AA 1844 vs 1846), and Haiku sits far below at 1620 ([Sonnet launch](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5), [Haiku launch](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-haiku-5-5)). ARC Prize, which is independent, shows **Opus and Fable tied on ARC-AGI-1 (98.5%) and ARC-AGI-2 (93.3% vs 93.8%)**, Sonnet clearly behind (83.9%) and Haiku far behind (61.3%). Only ARC-AGI-3 separates Fable (81.2%) from Sonnet (24.3%), and Opus has no entry there ([ARC Prize](https://arcprize.org/leaderboard)).

## Human judgment favours Opus, but the votes are thin

Collective human judgment exists only for the larger models, and none of it separates them decisively. On LMArena text, **Opus 5.5 (High) sits at 1514 ±9, statistically tied with Opus 4.6, Fable 5 and Gemini 4 Argon (1524 ±8)**. On WebDev, Opus leads Sonnet **1814 to 1772**, with no published confidence interval. Haiku 5.5 and Fable 5.2 have no Arena entries yet ([af.net](https://af.net/realtime/claude-opus-5-5-hits-1814-webdev-elo-as-standard-ai-benchmarks-falter/)). Design Arena's fullstack board ranks **Fable 5.2 first at 1400 and Opus second at 1388**, again without intervals ([benchmarklist mirror](https://benchmarklist.com/arenas/design_arena_fullstack/)). Usage share points the same way. On OpenRouter, Opus 5.5 is a top-six model at 6.98T weekly tokens. Within Anthropic's October mix, Opus ranked first, Sonnet second and Haiku third, with Haiku "a major driver" of token growth ([OpenRouter October](https://openrouter.ai/data/october-2026), [OpenRouter rankings](https://openrouter.ai/rankings)). The primary Arena pages returned HTTP 403, so every Arena figure here is secondary.

Developer reviews add texture rather than rankings. Zvi called the reactions to Opus 5.5 the "most consistently positive" he had collected. Complaints were overeagerness, about 1% destructive actions in one user's auto-mode use, and overthinking at max ([Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/claude-opus-55-should-raise-your)). The most useful human-graded comparison is CodeRabbit's review of 13 hard pull requests. **Sonnet 5.5 caught 6 bugs, Opus 8 at standard effort and 10 at max** ([laozhang.ai summary of CodeRabbit](https://blog.laozhang.ai/en/posts/claude-sonnet-5-5-vs-opus-5-5.md)). Small-sample reviewer tests went the other way on simpler coding. Sonnet passed 15/15 runs to Opus's 13/15 at max on three coding tasks, at $14.09 against $22.07 ([Lindy, relaying The New Stack](https://www.lindy.ai/blog/sonnet-5-5-vs-opus-5-5)). Fable 5.2, a week old, draws mixed reviews: "fast, creative, and finally not annoying" alongside reports of unchecked work and grammar slips ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/claude-fable-52-review-fast-creative), [corynezin](https://corynezin.bearblog.dev/im-not-impressed-with-fable-52/), [HN 50438506](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50438506)). One HN commenter's claim that **Fable high equals Opus max at about half the cost per task** agrees with the AA frontier above ([HN 50429068](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50429068)). Practitioners who route in production converge on the same division of labour: Opus or Fable plans and reviews, Haiku runs lookups and summaries, Sonnet handles exploration and small edits ([jock.pl](https://thoughts.jock.pl/p/anthropic-5-5-lineup-opus-haiku-max-credits-2026), [Kilo](https://blog.kilo.ai/p/your-weekly-limit-isnt-the-problem)).

The user's own sweep turned up slips that aggregate scores hide, and some favour the cheaper model. **Sonnet broke a 22-character limit at low, medium and high on localisation, and returned invalid JSON at medium; Haiku, Opus and Fable were right at low.** Sonnet low also misjudged an analytic task (67%) and doubled a $50 difference to $100. Haiku dropped duration or UTC times from an incident summary at every effort. Fable exceeded a summary word limit at low and medium ([own sweep matrix](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-antigravity-setup/5b430f7e-0f7b-55c5-8d67-00dd071d0571/scratchpad/bench/sweep/summary.txt)). With one run per cell these are signals, not verdicts. Still, they show that for strict-format work the cheapest model is not the riskiest one.

## The routing table: cheapest setting that matches the best result

Under the decision rule (quality first, then the cheapest setting that reaches the same result), the evidence supports the following routing. "Falls short" names the cheaper option that the evidence shows missing the mark. The strength labels are: **P** primary-verified independent, **S** secondary, **V** vendor-reported, **A** anecdotal, **O** own small-sample test.

| Task category | Route to | Cheaper option that falls short | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Read-only search, Explore subagent, file lookup | **Haiku medium** | none; higher Haiku effort only slower | S (36/36 sessions), O, V |
| Classification, extraction, routing, tagging | **Haiku low** | none found | O (100% all efforts), V |
| Strict format, JSON, length-capped strings, localisation | **Haiku low** | Sonnet low-high (length and JSON slips) | O |
| Short math, logic, probability, scheduling, capacity planning | **Haiku low** | none on easy items | O |
| Hard novel reasoning (ARC-AGI-2 class) | **Opus** (effort unpublished) | Sonnet (83.9 vs 93.3), Haiku (61.3) | P |
| SQL, data analysis, test writing, simple vision extraction | **Haiku low** | none found | O |
| Long-context retrieval under 100K tokens | **Haiku low** | none found; above 100K Haiku costs 5x | O, V |
| Summaries that must keep every fact | **Sonnet low** | Haiku (drops fields), Fable low/medium (word limit) | O |
| Small well-specified code change or app build | **Haiku medium**, verified by tests | Haiku low on long prompts (skips checks) | P (Vibe Code 90.4 vs 90.3), O, V |
| Multi-step terminal and repo coding, debugging | **Sonnet medium**, high if long | Haiku (36% vs 64-65%) | P (effort unlisted), V |
| Knowledge-work documents, reports, spreadsheets | **Sonnet medium-high** | Haiku (Elo 1620 vs 1844) | V, S |
| Building whole programs from scratch | **Opus or Fable** (high) | Sonnet (6.5% vs 18.5-19%) | P |
| SRE and operations agent work | **Opus** | Sonnet (30.2 vs 33.6), Haiku (5.7) | P |
| Hard code review and bug hunting | **Opus max** | Opus standard (8/13), Sonnet (6/13) | S, small n |
| Front-end UI judged by humans | **Opus** | Sonnet (WebDev 1772 vs 1814) | S |
| Multi-app business automation | **Fable xhigh**, max when critical | Sonnet max (44.6%), Opus max (40.0%) | P |
| Science, long-horizon ambiguous research | **Fable high** | Opus (TB-Science 47.1 vs 55.7) | P, V |
| Factual Q&A where errors are costly | **Fable high** | Opus (Omniscience 48 vs 64; 58.6% hallucination) | P, S |
| Computer use and GUI | **Opus** | Haiku (72.4), Sonnet (80.1) vs Opus 87.2 | V |
| Planning and architecture for others to execute | **Opus medium**, or Fable high on hard problems | none measured | A, V |

Several rows rest on a single source and need a note. The Haiku rows depend on keeping each request **under 100,000 tokens**. Above that line Haiku's price rises fivefold, and a Haiku main session crosses it after a few file reads, so Haiku belongs in subagents rather than in the main loop ([pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), [wmedia](https://wmedia.es/en/tips/claude-code-haiku-5-5-subagents)). Two fact-recall sources measure different things: AA-Omniscience at Fable high scores 64 and 77% accuracy, against Opus's 48 ([AA Omniscience](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/omniscience)), while the 58.6% hallucination rate for Opus comes from an aggregator using older data ([Suprmind](https://suprmind.ai/hub/ai-hallucination-rates-and-benchmarks/)). The Opus-or-Fable choice for whole-program builds is a cost tie. On the Vals Index, Fable was cheaper per test than Opus ($17.94 vs $24.77). On Terminal-Bench 4.0, Opus was cheaper ($12.04 vs $14.56). On the AA index, Fable high costs less than Opus max for more points, so **Fable high is the better default whenever a task already justifies Opus at xhigh or max**. Security work is a special case. Cyber-flagged requests on Fable and Opus re-run on Opus 4.8, and on Sonnet fall back to Sonnet 5, so the router cannot promise its chosen model on those tasks ([model config](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config), [Sonnet launch](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5)). Mythos 5.2 costs the same as Fable and beats it only marginally outside cyber and biology. It is limited to verified organisations, so it is not a routing target for most users ([models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview)).

## Subscription limits and cache rules shape the router more than price

Under tight subscription limits, three mechanics matter as much as the scores. First, **switching model mid-conversation cold-starts the prompt cache**, and changing top-level effort can invalidate it. Per-message effort, set through a system message, preserves the cache on all four models ([effort docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort)). OpenRouter's own router stays on one model for about 9 of 10 turns for the same reason ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/auto-and-auto-code-routers/)). A Claude Code router should therefore pick the model at session and subagent boundaries, through subagent `model:` and `effort:` frontmatter, `CLAUDE_CODE_SUBAGENT_MODEL` or `opusplan`, and vary effort within a session. Two configuration traps exist. Since v2.1.317, subagent effort follows the level saved for Haiku rather than the session's ([wmedia](https://wmedia.es/en/tips/claude-code-haiku-5-5-subagents)), and the top-level `effortLevel` user setting does not apply to Opus 5.5 ([model config](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config)).

Second, Haiku fan-out can cost more than it saves. In one measured pipeline, eight parallel Haiku searchers cost **51% more** than a single Sonnet searcher, because they returned four to ten times the text and inflated the main session's input by 66% ([AIVio Labs](https://www.aiviolabs.com/en/blog/haiku-5-5-as-a-subagent)). Subagents must return compact summaries. Third, limit structure differs by model. Max plans carry a weekly limit across all models plus **a separate weekly limit for Fable** ([Max plan](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11049741-what-is-the-max-plan)). Secondary sources say Fable can consume up to half the weekly allowance ([Kilo](https://blog.kilo.ai/p/your-weekly-limit-isnt-the-problem)). Fast mode bills usage credits even when plan usage remains, so a router should never enable it to save limits ([fast mode](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/fast-mode)).

Escalation needs a deterministic check to pay off. On AutomationBench, Opus's cost per *passed* task rises steadily with effort, from $6.95 at low to $10.54 at max, while the pass rate rises from 23% to 40% ([Zapier Opus report](https://zapier.com/benchmarks/claude-opus5_5-report)). "Try cheap, verify, escalate" therefore wins only when a test suite, schema validator or linter can detect the failure. Where no verifier exists, the rule of quality first argues for starting at the setting the table names. The Haiku warning about skipped checks at low effort ([effort docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort)) means a verifier should run outside the model, not rely on the model's own report that it checked.

## What the evidence cannot answer yet

Several routing-critical questions have no answer in the evidence:

- **Subscription weights.** No source gives the relative limit cost of each model, so the router must assume API price ratios or measure usage itself with `/usage` before and after controlled tasks.
- **Per-effort costs below max.** These are not primary-verified anywhere. An older AA snapshot gives costs roughly 40% lower than the current one ([Lindy](https://www.lindy.ai/blog/sonnet-5-5-vs-opus-5-5)), and Haiku's lower-effort costs are unpublished.
- **Effort curves for Sonnet and Haiku on agentic work.** None exist. Haiku is missing from AutomationBench, and the Vals Terminal-Bench run does not state effort.
- **Fable 5.2 in real use.** It is seven days old, with no independent Claude Code session costs, no Arena text entry and no measured writing-quality data.
- **Writing, translation and long-context behaviour above 100K tokens.** No independent measurements exist for any of the four models.
- **Cascade routing.** No published cascade or advisor experiment covers this model generation, so the savings from escalation are untested.
- **The user's own slips.** Sonnet's localisation failures, Haiku's dropped incident fields and Haiku's CLI miss (256,068 against 1,236,588) each rest on one run and need repeats before they become hard rules.
- **The top tier of the own tests.** The tests contain no task hard enough to separate Sonnet, Opus and Fable, so they cannot confirm any row above Sonnet in the routing table.

## Conclusion

The best single change for a usage-limited Claude Code setup is not a smarter choice among the big models. It is moving bounded subagent work to Haiku at medium and keeping the main loop on Sonnet at medium, which ties the top models on the strongest independent coding tests. Opus and Fable become exceptions, triggered by task type (hard review, whole-program builds, automation, science, costly facts) or by a failed verifier. The AA frontier suggests one counterintuitive rule: when a task justifies Opus at xhigh or max, Fable at high is cheaper per point. Effort deserves its own policy separate from model choice. Avoid max on Sonnet, xhigh on Fable and low on Opus, because each is beaten by another setting on both score and cost.

The weak link is not benchmark coverage but the missing price of limits. Every cost ratio here is an API ratio. Until the router measures real usage-window burn per model and effort on the user's own tasks, and repeats the harder own tests enough times to separate Sonnet from Opus, the routing table is a well-grounded default rather than a proven optimum.
